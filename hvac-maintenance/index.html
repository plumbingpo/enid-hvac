Why Hire a Professional for HVAC Maintenance?

Filters, a clear outdoor unit, open registers, and a quick look at the drain are the homeowner's share of maintenance, and they matter. Beyond those, it takes test instruments. Failures that leave you without heat or air rarely look like anything: an 80 percent capacitor looks like a healthy one, and a low charge is visible only in superheat and subcooling. A cracked heat exchanger, a lazy flame signal, a pressure switch near its limit, or an amp draw drifting toward the breaker rating all stay hidden until someone measures them, and each is less expensive to handle a season early.

How to Prevent HVAC Maintenance Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Clear the condensate drain early in cooling season, before algae has a chance to grow
  • Replace your filter on a one- to three-month cycle; nothing else you do pays off more
  • Trim plants back from the outdoor unit and give the coil a gentle rinse every spring
  • Keep supply and return registers open in every room, even the ones you rarely use
  • Check carbon monoxide alarms monthly in any home with fuel-burning appliances
  • Schedule maintenance in early spring and early fall, ahead of the busy season and the extreme weather
  • Pay attention to new noises or longer cycles and mention them at your next visit
